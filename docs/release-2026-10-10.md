# Six rounds of fixes: honest counts, no blind rounds, better results

## 1. The menus count what a match can deal

The home page said "149 places" and the map picker "100 unique places" for
SA × NL. Both counted the parked reconstruction plates and the photos pulled
as unfit, none of which a match deals. Now the dealer, the home footer, the
picker's counts and its city list all read one playable pool:

- **Without a Street View key:** 114 places; SA × NL is 69.
- **With a key:** 169 places; SA × NL is 110.
- **Cities:** only cities with a playable place are offered. Assen, whose
  only photo was unfit, no longer appears without a key. Urk and the other
  Street View towns appear with one.
- **A city picked earlier:** it stays listed, so it can be switched off.
- **Map previews:** the home and settings previews know when Street View is
  on.

Also in this round:

- **The city is named once.** "Grote Markt, Groningen" no longer repeats
  "Groningen" underneath. World places show city and country ("Agra ·
  India").
- **An Escape's results** list its five stops together, not as five
  one-row rounds.
- **"Total distance" is now "Total miss"**, so it doesn't read like the
  expedition's route length.

## 2. Street View: no blind rounds

- **A place that can't load is swapped.** If a Street View-only place can't
  load, a photo place from the same country takes its place before anyone
  answers. If the clock was running, it restarts, and a short notice says
  why. Solo, bot, pass-and-play and the online host make the swap; a guest
  gets it with the next update. These rounds used to be blind.
- **A failure belongs to its own place.** One place's Street View failure no
  longer carries over to the next, which used to get swapped before it had
  even tried to load.
- **Refused keys stop being tried.** Once Google refuses the key, photo
  places stop trying Street View.
- **A first-look hint:** "Drag to look around · use the arrows to walk"
  shows until the player first looks or moves.
- **The map's zoom button** at its limit no longer turns into a white block.

## 3. Accessibility

- **Focus moves with the screen.** It lands on each new screen, and on the
  results when a match ends. It used to stay on a button or map that had
  just disappeared.
- **Tab titles:** each screen has its own (Solo, Duel, Duel vs Grok, Pass
  and play, Room CODE).
- **Spoken warnings:** screen readers hear "10 seconds left" and "5 seconds
  left". The clock itself stays quiet.
- **Labels:** "Q 3 / 10" is read as "Question 3 of 10", and the photo
  controls and destination switcher are announced as groups.

## 4. Long matches and the reveal

- **The results lead with the next step.** Stats and Rematch / Share / Home
  come right after the score, map and passport. After a Classic, Rematch
  used to sit under forty rows.
- **Long matches fold by round.** Each round is one line (round, places,
  points, and your rival's points in a duel) that opens on demand.
- **The last card on a page always appears.** Scroll-in cards waited to
  clear the bottom 8% of the screen, which the last one never can.
- **"Tough one"** no longer adds "next round" mid-round.
- **Small neighbours' names wait for a closer zoom** (Belgium, Luxembourg,
  Lesotho, eSwatini, and Germany a little later). Their names no longer pile
  onto the answer pin after a far guess.

## 5. Online duels

- **A rematch is asked, not forced.** A guest's Rematch is a request. The
  host stays on the results and sees "*name* wants a rematch" with an
  **Accept rematch** button; accepting starts the new match at once. The
  guest sees "Asked *host* for a rematch". It used to throw the host out of
  the results the moment the guest pressed it.
- **A vanished host is noticed.** The guest listens for the host's regular
  update. After 10 seconds of silence it says "Lost touch with *host*.
  Waiting for them to come back…" and offers **Leave match**. A closed host
  tab used to leave the guest in a round that would never end.

## 6. The map's opening view

- **Both countries in view.** The world view now frames the lived-in band,
  so the Netherlands and South Africa are both in view on every screen: a
  360 px phone, the desktop corner map and landscape phones. South Africa
  used to sit half off the bottom.
- **The furthest zoom-out follows the map's width.** It shows the whole
  world once across: short panels can fit the world, and a wide expanded
  map no longer zooms out into empty sea.

## Checks

- **Tests:** 237 pass. Type checks, lint and the production build are clean.
  New tests cover:
  - what the menus count;
  - which cities are offered;
  - how the city is named;
  - the Street View swap: same country, no repeats, the same pick every
    time, the clock restarting, and never after a lock.
- **Browser:**
  - **Matches played through:** solo, bot and pass-and-play, Escape and a
    40-question Classic.
  - **Online duel in two browsers:** a guest reload mid-round, the rematch
    request and accept, and the host leaving.
  - **Keyboard:** a full match played with the keyboard only.
  - **axe:** every screen scanned, including the results; no violations.
  - **Screen sizes:** 360 px and 390 px phones, landscape, desktop.
  - **Production build:** served the way Vercel serves it.

## Later the same day: Street View on the live site

- **Settings:** settings and the home page now count your map honestly:
  "Your map has 169 places. Odyssey plays 80 of them, plus 20 in space."
  The old "100 unique places in your selected map" was the match length,
  and it counted space targets as places.
- **The clock waits for the scene.** The round's intro now holds until the
  photo, panorama or Street View is on screen, for up to 8 seconds, and
  says "Getting the place ready…". On a slow connection the round used to
  start over a dark, empty scene. That was worst right after a Street View
  swap, because the stand-in photo was never preloaded.
- **"Loading the photo…"** appears if a photo takes more than a moment.
- **Street View loads the way Google now expects:**
  - its classes come through `importLibrary("streetView")`;
  - the Google-only imagery filter is sent as `sources: [GOOGLE]`;
  - if Google rejects that filter, Street View falls back to outdoor
    imagery instead of failing everywhere.
- **The console says why Street View gave up.** For example: "no panorama
  within 5000 m", "request refused (…)", or a note pointing to Google's own
  error when the key is refused.
- **Settings → Picture shows the Street View status**:
  - **On**;
  - **off, because this build has no key**;
  - **off in this tab, because Google refused the key**: with Google's own
    reason (for example `RefererNotAllowedMapError`), what to change in
    Google Cloud, and a **Reload and try again** button.

  A refusal used to be silent: photos took over, and a reload wiped
  Google's reason from the console.
- **Street View works on the live site** once the key's website restriction
  includes `https://geo-gasser.vercel.app/*`.
- **The Photo switch works in duels.** The header's clear fade covered it
  whenever the two-row duel score card made the header taller, so clicks
  never reached it. The fade also stopped you dragging the scene near the
  top. Only the header's own buttons and cards take clicks now.
- **Street View no longer disappears when you switch back to it.**
  - **The switch waits for a panorama:** Street View / Photo appears once
    a panorama is found, not while the game is still looking. It used to
    let you cancel the search with Photo; the retry could then find
    nothing (a mountain top, say) and remove Street View and the switch
    mid-round.
  - **Switching back is instant:** the panorama stays loaded behind the
    photo, so Street View returns at once, where you left it, with no
    second search.

# Eight rounds: a game that celebrates

Motion all through the journey, a winner moment after every round, and the
bugs found on the way.

## 1. Every round ends with a winner

- **Duels:** rays turn, a flash goes off and confetti fires from both lower
  corners in the winner's colour. "YOU WIN ROUND 2" (or "GROK WINS ROUND 2")
  slams in with a shake, and a crown drops onto the winner's avatar. A tie
  reads "Dead heat".
- **Solo:** Escape stops show the verdict ("Perfect read · round 2
  complete"). Classic round summaries give a one-word verdict (Done, Solid,
  Great, Outstanding) and stamp in up to three stars: one from an average of
  6,000 a question, two from 10,000, three from 15,000.
- **Long names and verdicts** step down in size so they fit a 360 px phone.

## 2. The live round has a pulse

- **The last five seconds** thump over the scene, one big number a beat.
- **"GROK LOCKED IN!"** pops in when your rival locks while you are still
  thinking (any rival except pass-and-play, where the other player is at
  your shoulder).
- **Locking in** sends two rings out from your pin.

## 3. The reveal

- **Solo: your points fly** from the reveal card into the header score, and
  the total waits for them to land.
- **Streaks catch fire:** two or more rounds of 12,000+ in a row show a
  flaming "3 in a row" badge.
- **A close guess** no longer stacks the distance label on top of the
  answer pin.

## 4. The finale

- **A duel's winner wears the crown** on the results, whoever it is.
  Winning sets off confetti behind the results for a few seconds.
- **New personal best:** beating your record for the match length stamps
  "New personal best · Quick record was 20,000" under your score, with
  confetti. Records are now kept per match length, so a Quick is never
  measured against a Classic. Pass-and-play neither sets nor beats one.
- **Fixed:** an online guest's next match went unrecorded when the host
  started the rematch.

## 5. Home and menus

- **Fixed:** picking a map card on the home page faded it, and the card it
  replaced, out for good.
- **Fixed:** at 320 px the home header pushed the Settings bot half off
  screen.
- **Settings tabs** slide one highlight between them, and each section eases
  in. At 360 px the chosen tab's label no longer touches its edge; below
  340 px the tab icons step aside.
- **Picks pop:** a chosen map card rings and pops its tick; so do the
  settings options.
- **"Let's explore"** catches the light now and then, and your bot perks up
  and bobs as you type its name.

## 6. The duel room

- **The room code** flips in a letter at a time.
- **When your opponent sits down**, their row slides in with a ring and a
  whoosh, a VS stamp lands between you, and Start pulses.
- **Screen readers hear who joined** ("Guesty is here"). The room used to
  change in silence.
- **Copy invite** pops its tick.

## 7. Sweep

- **Keyboard:** starting a round with Enter puts focus on the map, ready
  for the arrow keys. It used to be fifteen Tabs away, behind the header
  buttons.
- **Landscape phones:**
  - the final-seconds number counts beside the map, not behind it;
  - the duel score card lays its two rows side by side, so the map no
    longer covers your rival's score;
  - on 360 px tall screens the map stops below the header.
- **Phone score card:** its tighter-row rule never applied (it looked for
  the wrong element role); it does now.
- **Reduced motion** (the setting, or the system setting alone) keeps the
  words, stars, crown and best stamp, and drops the confetti, rays and
  shake.

## Checks

- **Tests:** 248 pass (personal bests per length, round stars, streaks).
  Type checks, lint and the production build are clean.
- **axe:** no violations on any screen with the new motion: home, name
  prompt, all settings tabs, duel picker, the room (waiting, and joined
  for host and guest), the live round, the final five seconds, a rival
  lock, round splashes, reveals and the results with confetti.
- **Keyboard only:** solo and bot duel, from the first round to Rematch.
- **Online:** two browsers, the guest joining the host's room.
- **Screen sizes:** 320, 360 and 390 px phones; 667×375, 740×360, 844×390
  and 932×430 landscape; 1280 px desktop.
- **Known limit:** at 740×360 landscape the map's world view clips the
  "SOUTH AFRICA" label; the country itself is in view.

# Scoring the GeoGuessr way, with a points countdown

## Every round pays up to 5,000, the way GeoGuessr pays

- **The curve:** a guess scores `5000 × e^(−10 × distance / map size)`, and
  a pin within 25 m is a perfect 5,000. Rounds used to pay up to 20,000
  (10,000 for accuracy plus up to 10,000 for speed).
- **The map size** is measured the way GeoGuessr measures a map: the
  diagonal of its bounding box. South Africa is 2,135 km and the
  Netherlands 411 km; the world uses GeoGuessr's own 14,916.862 km. Each
  place is scored on its own country's map, so a 25 km miss costs more in
  the Netherlands (2,722) than in South Africa (4,448), and almost nothing
  on the world map (4,917).
- **No multipliers:** no round pays more than 5,000.

## The clock takes its share, in tiers

- **Five equal slices of the round.** A guess keeps all of its distance
  score in the first slice, then 90%, 80%, 70% and, in the last slice, 60%.
  On a 45 s clock the steps fall at 36, 27, 18 and 9 seconds left; Easy
  and Hard scale them to 60 s and 30 s.
- **Every guess scales the same way.** Speed can't rescue a far guess, and
  a perfect pin at the buzzer still earns 3,000. The old speed bonus is
  gone.

## A points countdown in the header

- **Under the clock, a strip shows what a perfect guess still earns:**
  5,000, then 4,500, 4,000, 3,500 and 3,000.
- **One cell per tier.** Spent tiers go dark; the live tier drains second
  by second toward the next step, in the screen's only accent colour.
- **On wider screens it names the next drop** ("−500 · 4S"). It reads
  "FLOOR" in the last tier, and "LOCKED" once you lock, frozen at your tier.
- **Screen readers hear each step once** ("4,000 points on offer"), not
  every second.
- **The round and question counter is one line of metadata**
  ("R 2/4 · Q 3/10", or "STOP 2/5" in an Escape). It replaces the round
  pips and the separate question line, so the photo keeps most of its
  height on phones.

## The reveal shows the sum

- **The breakdown is a ledger:** DISTANCE 4,368 · CLOCK ×0.9 · ROUND 3,931.
- **A note under it** names the tier you locked in: Locked in the "4,500"
  tier: the clock kept 90% of your 4,368.

## Built to the brief

The countdown, the ledger and the metadata line follow "The System Is the
Content":

- 1 px hairline joints, no shadows, no radius;
- Helvetica caps labels and monospaced tabular figures;
- one accent, reserved for the live tier and the round total;
- mechanical 120–150 ms steps with no spring.

The rest of the app keeps its current look; a full restyle would be a
separate decision.

## Everything that read the old scale

- **Rescaled:** round stars (3,750 / 2,500 / 1,500 average), the streak
  flame (3,000+ in a row), the share card's squares, the results map
  colours, the summary bars, and the bots' moods.
- **Records start again.** Personal bests set on the 20,000 scale can't be
  compared, so they are set aside. Matches, wins, streaks and distances
  carry over, and the home page shows a best once you set one.
- **"How to play"** explains the curve and the tiers.

# The Street View / Photo switch is always there

- **On every photo and Street View round, from the first second.** It used
  to appear only after a panorama was found, so it was missing while the
  search ran, on places with no panorama nearby, and on every place with
  no photo of its own.
- **Photo first.** Every place opens on its photo, and the next place
  opens on Photo again.
- **Street View says why it can't be pressed yet:** "Looking for Street
  View…", "No Street View near this place", or "Street View is off (see
  Settings, Picture)". It switches on as soon as a panorama is found.
- **Places with no photo** show their starting Street View frame, frozen,
  as the photo. Street View lets you walk; Photo freezes the start again,
  and Street View puts you back where you'd walked to. No extra Google
  loads.
- **Placement:**
  - landscape phones: in the scene left of the map, no longer over the map;
  - tablets and desktop: below the header, no longer over a duel's score
    card;
  - desktop reveal: top-left, clear of the result card;
  - phone reveal: hidden, because the result card and map cover the whole
    scene.

before it, with a colour change only.

- **Back:**

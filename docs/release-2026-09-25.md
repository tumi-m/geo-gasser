# Motion along the whole journey

Motion graphics from the first screen to the last, built from SVG strokes,
transforms and opacity — never a filter on text (see the 2026-09-23
results-screen blur fix). Every piece settles to a still final frame, so with
Reduce Motion the journey shows its end states (and the splashes cross-fade).

## Home
- **Hero tours its destinations**: every 7s the photo cross-fades to the next
  (all three stay mounted, so it is a fade, not a cut) with a slow camera
  drift; a lime line on the active thumbnail fills to show the next change.
  Pauses on hover or focus, stops once you pick one, off with reduced motion.
- **Headline** words rise out of their line; a brush stroke draws itself under
  "unexpected."; the status dot pings.
- **Flight path**: a dashed route draws across the photo and a pin keeps
  making the trip (SVG `animateMotion`, which works everywhere SVG does; not
  rendered with reduced motion), landing on a pulsing marker. Phones get their
  own route through the open band above the destination name.
- Atlas cards rise in as they scroll into view.

## Between screens
- Route changes cross-fade (old screen eases back, new one rises) via the
  router's View Transitions support; browsers without it swap as before.

## Round intro (`round-intro.tsx`)
- A title card instead of static text: a compass draws its ring and ticks,
  the needle swings past north and settles, the round number rolls up in a
  lime badge, "Locate this" rises in, and chips for the clock and atlas slide
  in. A ring around the compass fills for exactly the hold (2.2s; 1.2s with
  reduced motion), then the round starts itself. Tap or Enter starts it now.

## During a round
- The photo develops in when it loads (fade plus one sweep of light).
- Your pin sends out a ripple each time it lands somewhere new.
- Last ten seconds: the clock beats each second and its ring glows.
- Locking in before your opponent: a ring and check draw themselves with a
  "Locked in" stamp, then clear; the waiting chip gets breathing dots.

## Final results
- The winner's avatar pops with a ring pulse, the name rises in, the score
  counts up with a burst; round cards cascade in as you scroll; stat tiles
  count up when they come into view; Rematch glows after a few seconds.

## Fixed along the way
- **The last ten seconds stopped looking urgent once the other player
  locked.** Urgency was tied to the `round_active` phase, but a duel moves to
  `waiting_for_opponent` as soon as either player locks — while your clock
  keeps running. Urgency now follows the same rule as the clock.
- A hydration warning on the music link field: Chromium autofill adds an
  empty `style` attribute before hydration; suppressed on that field only.

## Verification
Browser, 390 × 844 and 1280 × 800: the hero tour advances after 7s, the pin
travels, cards reveal; the intro starts itself at 2.2s; the photo sheen, pin
ripples (drop and move), the timer heartbeat after Grok locked first, the
Locked-in stamp and its clearing; a full solo game to results with every card
revealed and stats counted. The same journey with reduced motion. The
5-round splash suite and the hydration check on all five pages re-run clean.

# Player bots

The flat, muted avatars are replaced by living bots, modelled on Grok's
"Create New Bot" builder and taken further.

- **Look**: 8 bodies (circle, blob, square, pill, triangle, hexagon, cloud,
  drop) in 12 colours — Grok's eleven plus the game's lime — each with a soft
  light-and-shade finish and a face of two rounded strokes placed to suit the
  body. 96 combinations. Grok keeps its identity: an ink hexagon with white
  eyes and its orange star.
- **Behaviour**: live bots breathe, blink and glance around on their own
  rhythm (derived from a hash of the id, so no two bots are in step and the
  server and client agree). Changing shape or colour makes the bot pop into
  its new body with a happy face. In the builder its eyes follow your finger
  or cursor.
- **Moods**: neutral "//", happy "/\\", sad "\\/", surprised (wide round
  eyes), focused (narrowed). In a round splash both bots concentrate while
  the points count, then the winner beams and the loser droops. Your HUD bot
  looks surprised in the last ten seconds and happy once you lock in; the
  winner on the final results is happy.
- **Builder** (`AvatarBuilder`): bodies drawn in your current colour (4 × 2),
  colours (6 × 2), and Shuffle. It is in the first-play prompt ("build your
  bot and name it"), the duel page (for you and a pass-and-play guest), and
  Settings.
- Ids are `shape-colour` (≤ 15 chars; the wire allows 24). First-generation
  ids (`atlas`, `canal`, …) map to new bots, so saved settings and older peers
  still render; anything else falls back to the default. Five unit tests.

# Photos: fill the frame and look around

Reported: blurred bars either side of the photo, the picture too small, and
no way to look left or right for detail.

- **Bars and small photos**: "whole photo" was the default, so on a phone a
  landscape or tall plate shrank to fit and the rest of the frame was filled
  with a blurred copy. The default is now to fill the frame. Players who had
  the old default move to it once (`photoFitChosen` records an explicit
  choice from now on); "Full photograph" is still in Settings and the photo
  controls for anyone who picks it.
- **Dragging went the wrong way**: the viewer used look semantics (drag right,
  the picture moves left). The photo now follows the finger pixel for pixel,
  on both axes. Keyboard look keys keep their meaning.
- **Filled photos could not really be panned**: the image element was the
  size of the frame, cropped by `object-fit`, so the parts past the frame were
  never drawn — the pan bounds assumed they were, and dragging slid the photo
  off to show background. The element is now sized to the whole scaled plate.
  Measured: dragged hard into either corner, or zoomed to 2.2×, the photo
  still covers the frame.
- **Showing that there is more**: when a plate reaches past the frame by more
  than 24px, the view pans out to one edge, across to the other and back once
  the round starts (any touch, key or wheel stops it); the hint shows which
  way to drag. Double-tap zooms to 2.2× on the spot you tapped; again zooms
  out.
- The photo gets more of a phone screen: the map sheet is 49% of the height
  (was 55%), so the frame on a 390 × 844 phone is 328px tall (was 278).

Verified on three plates (3:2 sweeps sideways by 50px each way; 4:3 overhangs
24px, no sweep; a portrait plate sweeps up and down by 96px): drag follows
the finger, double-tap in and out, no background at any edge. The chosen
"Full photograph" still shows every edge; desktop still fills the screen; a
full game plays through clean.

# A fair map, closer looks, fifty new places and a dotted Earth

## The map no longer points at answers (competitive fix)

The map's town list had been built from the answers themselves:
- Agra's dot sat on the Taj Mahal, Petra's on the Treasury, Zermatt's on the
  Matterhorn.
- 103 of 149 answers had a dot or search result within 1.5 km, most at
  exactly 0 m.
- In a country with one location (India, Jordan, Iceland…) the only dot was
  the answer.
- Searching a landmark ("taj", "golden gate", "muiderslot") pinned it
  exactly.

What changed:
- **Independent towns.** Dots, labels and search now come from GeoNames town
  centres (CC BY 4.0), built by `scripts/build-places.mjs`: 321 South
  African and 412 Dutch towns and about 3,800 world cities. Every country
  keeps at least its eight largest towns; India alone has 210.
- **Revealed by size.** Places appear by size as you zoom. Dots paint on one
  canvas in their own pane, and labels are culled to the view and never
  overlap.
- **City search only.** Search covers towns by name, nickname ("joburg",
  "den haag") and former name ("port elizabeth"). There are no landmark
  aliases.
- **Tests.** Every answer country has at least eight dots, and no world
  landmark sits within a kilometre of a dot that isn't its own town.
- **Date line.** Russia and Fiji crossed the date line and drew bands of land
  across the zoomed-out world map. Their outlines now split at 180°.

## Looking closer

- **Magnifier.** Press and hold the photo, or switch on the magnifier, for a
  loupe at 2.6×. On touch it floats above your finger.
- **Zoom toward the cursor.** The scroll wheel zooms toward the cursor, not
  the centre.
- **Sharper zoom.** Where a 3,840 px companion photo exists, it swaps in once
  you zoom past what the base photo holds (fully decoded, no flash), and zoom
  goes to 4.5×.
- **Viewpoints.** Places can carry extra viewpoints. Step between them with
  the arrows in the photo controls, or Q and E.
- **Focus pull.** Photos arrive out of focus and pull sharp as they fade in.

## Fifty new places, waiting on photos

12 in South Africa, 12 in the Netherlands and 26 around the world
(`src/lib/game/new-locations.ts`):
- India gains three places, and Japan and China two each.
- Seven new countries join: Austria, Belgium, Denmark, Malaysia, Namibia,
  Poland and the UAE.

A place switches on only once its photo is in
`src/lib/game/photo-manifest.ts`, so no round is ever dealt without a
picture.

The photos come from Wikimedia Commons. This build environment's network
policy blocks `commons.wikimedia.org` and `upload.wikimedia.org`, so they
aren't downloaded yet. Where those hosts are reachable:

```
node --experimental-strip-types scripts/fetch-new-photos.mjs            # the 50 new places
node --experimental-strip-types scripts/fetch-new-photos.mjs --views    # 2 more viewpoints for every existing place
node --experimental-strip-types scripts/fetch-new-photos.mjs --upgrade  # sharper photos for the 18 under 1400 px
node scripts/build-hires-manifest.mjs
```

What the script does:
- Takes candidates from each place's Commons category, a Commons search and
  a geosearch around the coordinates.
- Keeps large landscape photos under CC BY, CC BY-SA, CC0 or public domain,
  with Featured and Quality images first.
- Saves a 1,920 px photo, a 3,840 px companion for zooming, and up to two
  more viewpoints.
- Credits every photographer.

Review the photos before shipping.

## Motion across the journey

- **Dotted Earth.** A canvas globe made of 2,400 land dots (13 KB, loaded on
  demand, no WebGL or texture). It has an atmosphere rim, pulsing places, and
  arcs that rise off the surface with a comet at the tip. It only ever knows
  the atlas, never a question.
  - **Round intro:** the title card sits on the globe, turned toward the
    atlas in play.
  - **Lobby:** the world turns behind the room while you wait.
  - **Home:** a small globe beside "Choose your playground" turns to face the
    map you pick.
- **Passport stamps.** On the results screen, every country the match visited
  gets an ink stamp at its own angle, landing one by one.
